As a [dance music fan](/blog/everybody-in-the-place), and [occasional DJ](https://soundcloud.com/syntax-terror-exe), there's a somewhat related hobby I've avoided until recently. It starts out small, first with one purchase, then a few, then all of a sudden you realise you've spent hundreds or even thousands of pounds on synths and drum machines.

Now that I'm approaching my 40s, it was only a matter of time before I got into music hardware. There seems to be an unwritten rule of raving: if you spend your 20s and 30s on dancefloors, you mix up your fourth decade by spending time in your office/studio pressing buttons and twiddling knobs to smash out bangers that, chances are, only you will hear.

It started with the [PO-12](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=ssT4YVTNAQ4) from [Teenage Engineering](https://teenage.engineering/), who also designed the [Playdate](/blog/surprise-and-delight/). TE are an _Apple-like_ company in more ways than one. To some, they make beautiful electronic devices that are themselves like a piece of art. To others, they're overpriced and underpowered pieces of kit that only sell well because they look nice. The PO-12 lingered on my desk for a few years, occasionally being played at the end of a work day, but after recently plugging it into some proper speakers the sound difference over the built-in speaker showed me what was possible.

Soon after, I found their [EP-133 K.O. II](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=AcheyH_bXRQ) going for 100 quid on eBay. An absolute steal, so I got a last-minute bid in and won it.

And now that I have researched these products, my YouTube algorithm is pushing music hardware at me, so when I saw a [£65 synth that lets you customise the firmware](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=cM1ubk7JQ3E), I knew I had to buy it.

While I was eagerly awaiting the FM-1's long trip from China, I was dropping into various second-hand shops to see if they had anything interesting. Imagine my luck when I found a [Korg Volca Drum](https://www.youtube.com/shorts/vAPIi6vxW2s) in a Cash Converters for 60 quid.

So now, I have a pretty decent starter collection of music hardware. I just have to figure out how to use them all.

## Twisting knobs

For years, practically since 2020, I've been trying to find more hobbies that don't involve screens. DJing and music production require my laptop, and I love playing video games, but those are on a screen.

Music hardware feels like a puzzle in a game. I'm experimenting through play, memorising techniques, and building a mental model of how they work.

There's also a physicality to it that screens can't convey. The real controls hit some dopamine centre in my brain that clicking a digital button just doesn't.

Turns out there's a name for this: [Tactile Technology](https://www.theambony.com/lifestyle/teenage-engineering-tp-7-and-the-return-of-tactile-technology).

When everything is on a touch device or a screen, we lose a part of that tactile relationship with technology. I'm at the start of this new hobby, and it's already fun to press buttons, twiddle knobs, and click switches, to make music happen.

But I've been told I'm not allowed to buy any more until I can do a live set. I'll not mention that I've already bought another.
