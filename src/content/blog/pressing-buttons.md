As a [dance music fan](/blog/everybody-in-the-place), and [occasional DJ](https://soundcloud.com/syntax-terror-exe), there's a somewhat related hobby I've avoided until recently. It starts out small, first with one purchase, then a few, then all of a sudden you realise you've spent hundreds or even thousands of pounds on synths and drum machines.

It was only a matter of time, as I approach my 40s, that I get into music hardware. There seems to be some unwritten rule of raving, that if you spend your 20s and 30s on dancefloors, your fourth decade is spent in your office/studio pressing buttons and twiddling knobs to smash out bangers that chances are, only you may hear.

It started with the [PO-12](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=ssT4YVTNAQ4) from [Teenage Engineering](https://teenage.engineering/), who also designed the [Playdate](/blog/surprise-and-delight/). TE are an _Apple-like_ company in more ways than one. To some, they make beautiful electronic devices that are themselves like a piece of art. To others, it's overpriced and underpowered that only sells well because it looks nice. The PO-12 lingered on my desk for a few years, occasionally being played at the end of a work day, but after recently plugging it into some proper speakers the sound difference over the built in speaker showed me what was possible.

Soon after, I found their [EP-133 K.0. II](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=AcheyH_bXRQ) going for 100 quid on eBay. An absolute steal, so I got a last minute bid in and won it.

And now that I have researched these products, my YouTube algorithm is pushing music hardware at me, so when I saw a [£65 synth that let's you customise the firmware](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=cM1ubk7JQ3E), I knew I had to buy it.

While I eagerly awaited the FM-1's long trip from China I've been dropping into various 2nd hand shops to see if they have anything interesting. Imagine my luck, when I found a [Korg Volca Drum](https://www.youtube.com/shorts/vAPIi6vxW2s) in a CashConverters for 60 quid.

So now, I have a pretty decent starter collection of music hardware. I just have to figure out how to use them all.

## Twisting knobs

For years, practically since 2020, I've been trying to find more hobbies that don't involve screens. DJing and music production require my laptop, and I love playing videogames, but those are on a screen. 

Music hardware feels like a puzzle in a game. I'm experimenting through play, memorising techniques, and building a mental model in your head of how they are work.

There's also a physicality to it that screens can't convey. The real controls hit some dopamine centre in my brain that clicking a digital button just doesn't. 

Turns out there's a name for this - [Tactile Technology](https://www.theambony.com/lifestyle/teenage-engineering-tp-7-and-the-return-of-tactile-technology). 

When everything is on a touch device or a screen, we lose a part of that more fun relationship with technology.  I'm at the start of this new hobby, and it's already fun to press buttons and twiddle knobs and make music happen. 

But I've been told I'm not allowed to buy any more until I can do a live set.
