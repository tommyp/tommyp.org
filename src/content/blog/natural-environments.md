Right now, London is in a drought. The signs of it are everywhere.

London is filled with parks, both huge and tiny. The grass has turned a crispy brown, prompting warnings of wildfires.

The pavement gunk, probably made from tears of pub goers when they realise the price of the pint they just spilled, would normally be washed away after a few days. Instead it has became noticable enough [it has mystified some people on Reddit](https://www.reddit.com/r/london/comments/1va2sbw/whats_going_on_with_the_pavements_in_the_city_and/).

We're also in a hosepipe ban. I've always heard of these but it hasn't happened in London since I moved here. It didn't stop my neighbour from pressure washing his car and his children's bikes yesterday though.

Today I finished the couch to 5k for the 3rd time. My route takes me round the resovoirs and canals forming the Walthamstow Wetlands and it's nearby waterways. Those resovoirs are the lowest I've ever seen since we've lived here.

## Wildflowering

In recent years, ecological restoration concepts have entered the public conciousness. We've tried to do our part in our garden by adding a variety of plants, throwing down wilderflower seeds, planting trees, and Lila's taken to feeding the birds every morning and despite the 5am wake ups from gangs of starlings, sparrows, and magpies, it's lovely to see many different species of birds make a visit.

We recently went to Norway and we came back to our garden looking more like a wild meadow than a garden in London. We hadn't bothered cutting it before we went, and the heatwaves left it in a state that some might describe as overgrown. We loved it. We've since spent afternoons working in the garden while watching bees, butterflies, birds come and go.
